The Problem

Building Rural? You've Got a Fire Safety Decision to Make.

If your new home is located beyond fire service coverage in rural New Zealand, the Building Code requires you to provide adequate firefighting water. The default option is a dedicated 45,000-litre water tank — plus earthworks, plumbing, and a chunk of your property.

The smarter alternative is a residential fire sprinkler system that protects your family from inside your ceiling, meets code requirements, and costs significantly less.

Most homeowners discover this requirement mid-way through the consent process. What should be an exciting build turns into a stressful compliance scramble.

Fire Sprinklers Save Lives, Property, and Money

Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) strongly recommends home fire sprinkler systems for any dwelling more than 10 minutes from a fire station. The evidence is overwhelming:

  • verified_user Residential fire sprinklers control fires approximately 96% of the time.
  • person_off Occupants in sprinkler-protected homes are roughly 80% less likely to die in a house fire.
  • home_work Property damage is reduced by up to 70% compared to unprotected homes.
  • water_drop Sprinklers use 40–80 litres/min vs 700+ litres/min from fire service hoses — far less water damage.
  • timer A home fire doubles in size every 30 seconds. Sprinklers buy at least 10 extra minutes of escape time.
  • safety_check Combined with interconnected smoke alarms, sprinklers reduce fire death risk by approximately 82%.

Modern residential sprinklers are nothing like bulky industrial heads. Concealed heads sit flush behind a 60mm cover plate — barely noticeable, designed to match your ceiling.

How They Work

Residential fire sprinklers are activated by heat (~68°C), not smoke. Only the head directly above the fire activates. The rest stay sealed.

Everyone Hears the Alarm. Every Time.

A standalone smoke alarm only sounds in the room where it detects smoke. Interconnected alarms change this: when one detects smoke, every alarm sounds simultaneously.

Common Questions

Do I need to replace the sprinkler heads over time? expand_more
No expiry date. They last the lifetime of the home unless damaged or painted over. Regular visual checks are all that's needed.
What if the sprinklers accidentally go off? expand_more
Extremely rare. They are heat-activated (~68°C), not smoke-activated. Burning toast won't trigger them.
How reliable are residential fire sprinklers? expand_more
~96% effectiveness. It's a simple mechanical system with no electronics to fail.
Do I have to pay for maintenance each year? expand_more
No mandatory professional annual service. Homeowner checks pressure gauge annually. Professional check recommended every 3-5 years.
Do all the sprinklers go off at once? expand_more
No. Only the one above the fire. Hollywood gets this wrong.
Can my existing plumber install the system? expand_more
Yes. It is plumbing work. We design the system and train your plumber.
